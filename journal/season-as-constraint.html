Season Is Not a Limitation, It Is the Idea

Every restaurant has a constraint. Most choose the wrong one. Price, square footage, and spring asparagus in October are all just choices about what to lie about.

Published August 30, 2026 Read 5 min By Ada Roerig

A few Tuesdays ago, in the last week of the asparagus season, the field report came in at nine in the morning: half the usual case. By eleven the report was amended: none. We printed the tasting menu without asparagus at noon, and the second course that night, built in two hours out of whatever the walk-in was proudest of, was the best plate of the month. Guests asked what it was. The answer was the truth: the asparagus ran out, so here is what the valley actually had.

Every kitchen has a constraint. The honest ones choose theirs. Ours is the season, and saying that out loud is the entire sourcing philosophy. When asparagus is over, it is over, and the menu does not pretend otherwise. The alternative is a phone call to a wholesaler and asparagus flown from somewhere it is still technically spring, which is not a menu decision, it is a decision about what the menu is allowed to say.

Constraints get a bad name because people confuse them with restrictions. A restriction shrinks the field of play. A constraint shapes it. Ours has produced a kitchen that is better than it would have been with unlimited access, because a cook who can have anything practices nothing. Twenty years from now the expensive part of this industry will not be the protein, it will be the trust, and trust is stocked at farms, not at markets.

The counterargument is the guest who wanted the asparagus. It is a fair hit. They came on the wrong Tuesday, through no fault of their own, and the menu is a promise we made in July about October. Our answer is the field report itself: we post it every Monday on the journal, so the person planning a birthday in late October can see that the menu will be root vegetables and decide accordingly. Constraint and candor together beat neither alone. A menu that lies about its season is worse than one that is honest about being limited, and a kitchen that is honest and limited is the only kind we can run without flinching at our own sign.

End
