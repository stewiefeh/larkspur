The story

First the barn,
then the restaurant

Larkspur opened in the spring of 2021 in a 1904 apple barn on Front Street. The chef came for the hearth. The hearth came with the barn.

Opened May 2021 Chef Ada Roerig Room 47 seats

Ada Roerig cooked in Copenhagen for nine years and came home to the Hudson Valley to do one thing: build a restaurant where the fire is the stove and the market is the menu. No reservationist scripts, no menu engineering, no sous vide circulators. A barn, a hearth, eleven farms, and enough hours in the evening to cook properly.

The name is for the flower that grows in the gaps of the barn's foundation wall, the one the masons refused to clear because it bloomed every year regardless. That seemed like the right standard.

"We don't have a menu concept. We have a valley, and the valley has opinions."

The timeline

Five years, one idea

Spring 2019

The barn moves

The 1904 structure comes down the river on a flatbed and goes back up stone by stone on Front Street. The foundation wall keeps its larkspurs.

May 2021

First service

Thirty-one seats, no bar, no website. A chalkboard menu that changed mid-service when the call came in from the farm.

2023

The tasting goes to four

The à la carte menu was doing more harm than good. The kitchen cut it to the four-movement format and never looked back. The bar kept a la carte.

2024

The river wall opens

The window wall gets finished and the room stops being a secret. First three-star review arrives. The team doubles to nineteen.

2026

The long table

Nine seats at a single oak slab, parties of eight, one menu, no choices. It books out a season ahead and it is the best seat in the house.
