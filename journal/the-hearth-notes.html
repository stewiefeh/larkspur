Notes From the Hearth, Month One

Fire is a colleague with opinions. Month one of cooking over coals only.

Published July 14, 2026 Read 7 min By Ada Roerig

The hearth went gas-free on the first of July, and the first month of cooking over live coals rearranged more in the kitchen than the previous five years combined. A gas range answers in seconds. A coal bed answers in quarters of an hour, and it has moods, and you negotiate with it three services a day until it feels less like equipment and more like the senior-most member of staff, which by heat-hours it effectively is.

The first lesson is zone discipline. One fire is three ovens: the bright end for searing, the middle for roasting, the edge for holding. The old station maps put protein in one corner and vegetables in another; the new map puts every dish on a journey across the floor of the hearth, timed to the fire's slow decline. The duck starts at the bright end and finishes at the edge while the room fills. By service's end the same grate has cooked a tasting menu, which is the kind of efficiency no equipment catalog advertises because nobody can sell it to you.

The second lesson is that smoke is an ingredient with a concentration curve. The first hour of a fire gives you whispers, the third gives you declarations. We learned to match the fire's hour to the dish instead of treating smoke as a constant. The bread wants hour three, when the coals are mature and the smoke has backbone. The scallops want hour one, when the smoke is more rumor than flavor. Write both on the board and the station stops being superstition.

The third lesson is about the cooks. A hearth station is a physical education. You read fire with your hands over it the way you read a sauce with a spoon, and that skill has no shortcut, only reps. What surprised us is how quickly the young cooks took to it and how much calmer the line became. A gas line is a waiting machine. A fire is a living one. You attend to it, and the attending keeps you honest in a way that dials never asked us to be.

Month one ends with the fire undefeated and the kitchen converted. The gas line is capped. The menu has never been more specific to one room in one building in one valley, which was the point all along.
